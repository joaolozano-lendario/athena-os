## USAGE NOTES

### When to Use Creative-Context

- Outcome is not predetermined
- Multiple valid solutions exist
- Novelty/originality is valued
- Lateral thinking required
- Aesthetic or emotional dimensions matter
- Strategic positioning work

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Product Naming

```markdown
## PERSONA
You are a naming strategist specializing in developer tools, with a portfolio including successful launches in the observability and security spaces. You understand that developers value clarity over cleverness, but appreciate subtle wit.

## INSPIRATION

### Reference Points
- Sentry (evokes vigilance without aggression)
- Honeycomb (suggests structure and insight)
- LaunchDarkly (playful but purposeful)

### Style Influences
- One or two words maximum
- Avoids forced acronyms
- Pronunciation obvious from spelling
- Suggests benefit without being literal

### Anti-Patterns (What to Avoid)
- Generic tech suffixes (-ly, -ify, -io overuse)
- Misspellings for domain availability (e.g., "Konveyor")
- Aggressive/militaristic terminology (e.g., "CodeBlitz", "BugAssault")
- Cutesy animal names without metaphorical fit

## OBJECTIVE
Generate 15-20 naming options for a static analysis tool that finds security vulnerabilities in infrastructure-as-code (Terraform, CloudFormation, Kubernetes YAML). Target users are DevOps engineers and SREs who value precision and automation.

## EXPLORATION SPACE

### Boundaries (Hard Constraints)
- Available .com or .dev domain
- Pronounceable in English (primary market)
- No existing trademarks in software/security space
- Safe for workplace conversation (no double entendres)

### Freedoms (Encouraged Experimentation)
- Neologisms welcome if intuitive
- Technical metaphors encouraged (astronomy, biology, engineering)
- Can play with security/safety concepts
- Open to compound words

### Target Audience
Senior DevOps engineers (5+ years experience), SREs, security engineers in cloud-native orgs. Skeptical of marketing hype, value tools that "just work".

### Tone & Voice
Competent, reliable, low-drama. More "trusted colleague" than "vigilant guardian". Suggests prevention over detection.

## CREATIVE CONSTRAINTS

### Must Have
- Implies security, safety, or correctness
- Feels modern but not trendy
- Easy to verb ("We should [name] this before deploying")

### Should Have
- 2 syllables or fewer per word
- Suggests automation or intelligence
- Differentiated from existing tools (Checkov, TFSec, Terrascan)

### Nice to Have
- Subtle reference to infrastructure/cloud concepts
- Memorable without being gimmicky
- Positive connotations (building up vs tearing down)

## OUTPUT STYLE

### Format
Table with columns: Name | Rationale (1 sentence) | Domain Status | Trademark Risk (Low/Med/High)

### Quantity
15-20 options, organized into 3 thematic groups.

### Evaluation Lens
Rate each name on 1-5 scale for:
- Clarity (does it suggest purpose?)
- Memorability (will people remember it?)
- Differentiation (stands out from competitors?)
```

### Example 2: Content Strategy Ideation

```markdown
## PERSONA
You are a content strategist who's built thought leadership programs for B2B SaaS companies transitioning from product-led to enterprise sales. You understand the balance between educational content and demand generation.

## INSPIRATION

### Reference Points
- Stripe's "Increment" magazine (high-quality, practitioner-focused)
- AWS's "This is My Architecture" series (customer storytelling)
- Gong's data-driven blog posts (research + narrative)

### Style Influences
- Show don't tell (data, case studies, not claims)
- Practitioner voice (peer-to-peer, not vendor-to-buyer)
- Format innovation (interactive, visual, multi-modal)

### Anti-Patterns (What to Avoid)
- Thinly-veiled product pitches
- Generic "best practices" listicles
- Gated content with low value exchange
- Trend-chasing without domain fit

## OBJECTIVE
Design a 6-month content program for a database monitoring startup targeting senior database administrators and data platform engineers at Series B+ companies. Goal: establish category authority and generate qualified enterprise leads.

## EXPLORATION SPACE

### Boundaries (Hard Constraints)
- Budget: $150K total (production + promotion)
- Team: 1 content lead, 1 designer, freelance writers
- Cannot do live events (team bandwidth)
- Must support sales pipeline metrics (SQLs, not just traffic)

### Freedoms (Encouraged Experimentation)
- Any content format (written, video, interactive, data viz)
- Open to controversial takes (within professional bounds)
- Can involve customers, partners, or third-party experts
- Paid promotion acceptable

### Target Audience
DBAs and data engineers who've experienced production database incidents. Pain-aware, evaluating solutions. Value deep technical content over marketing fluff.

### Tone & Voice
Empathetic expert. "We've been in the trenches." Data-driven but not academic. Opinionated but not dogmatic.

## CREATIVE CONSTRAINTS

### Must Have
- At least 3 content pillars (thematic areas)
- Mix of formats (not all blog posts)
- Measurement framework (leading + lagging indicators)
- Distribution strategy per content piece

### Should Have
- Original research or data (not just commentary)
- Customer involvement (quotes, case studies, co-creation)
- SEO consideration (but not SEO-first)
- Sales enablement component (how will sales use this?)

### Nice to Have
- Interactive/experiential elements
- Community engagement angle
- Content that compounds (evergreen + updatable)
- Cross-functional collaboration (eng, product, customer success)

## OUTPUT STYLE

### Format
For each content pillar:
1. Pillar Name & Thesis (2-3 sentences)
2. Content Ideas (3-5 per pillar with format + distribution)
3. Success Metrics
4. Resource Allocation

Include an editorial calendar skeleton (monthly themes).

### Quantity
3 content pillars, 10-15 total content ideas.

### Evaluation Lens
For each idea, assess:
- Demand Potential (search volume, social interest)
- Differentiation (can competitors easily replicate?)
- Production Feasibility (within budget/timeline?)
- Sales Alignment (supports deal conversations?)
```

### Example 3: Workshop Facilitation Design

```markdown
## PERSONA
You are a workshop designer specializing in strategic offsites for executive teams. Your workshops are known for creating clarity without being prescriptive, using frameworks that stick, and generating actionable outcomes (not just "insights").

## INSPIRATION

### Reference Points
- Amazon's "Working Backwards" press release method
- Strategyzer's Business Model Canvas workshops
- IDEO's design thinking sprints
- Atlassian's "Team Playbook" exercises

### Style Influences
- Timeboxed exercises (forcing functions)
- Tangible artifacts (teams leave with something)
- Structured divergence before convergence
- Visual/spatial thinking tools

### Anti-Patterns (What to Avoid)
- Death by sticky notes (analog for analog's sake)
- Endless brainstorming without synthesis
- Exercises that require "you had to be there" to understand output
- Generic team-building disguised as strategy work

## OBJECTIVE
Design a 4-hour workshop for a Series A startup's leadership team (CEO, CTO, VP Sales, VP Product, Head of CS) to align on their Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) after experiencing product-market fit drift. Outcome: clear ICP definition that informs product roadmap and go-to-market strategy.

## EXPLORATION SPACE

### Boundaries (Hard Constraints)
- 4 hours max (half-day offsite)
- 5 participants (cannot split into breakout groups)
- Remote-first (must work on Zoom + Miro/Figma)
- Must produce artifact usable in Board meeting next week

### Freedoms (Encouraged Experimentation)
- Any facilitation techniques (canvases, role-play, simulations)
- Can use customer data/interviews as inputs
- Open to async pre-work (if <30 min per person)
- Creative artifact formats (not just slides)

### Target Audience
Experienced operators (2nd/3rd-time execs). Low tolerance for fluff. Want frameworks they can reuse. Need to get back to "real work" after workshop.

### Tone & Voice
Rigorous but not academic. Fast-paced but not rushed. Facilitator as "guide on the side" not "sage on stage."

## CREATIVE CONSTRAINTS

### Must Have
- Clear ICP definition (firmographics + psychographics)
- Prioritization of multiple customer segments
- Alignment on "who we're NOT serving"
- Action items with owners for next 2 weeks

### Should Have
- Customer evidence/data integration (not just opinions)
- Mechanisms for resolving disagreement
- Connection to downstream decisions (pricing, product, marketing)
- Visual artifact (not just text document)

### Nice to Have
- Reusable framework for future ICP reviews
- Customer empathy-building component
- Quantification (TAM implications of ICP choices)
- Alignment on language/terminology

## OUTPUT STYLE

### Format
Workshop run-of-show with:
1. Agenda (time blocks with exercise names)
2. Exercise details (objective, instructions, materials, output)
3. Facilitation notes (common pitfalls, time warnings)
4. Artifact template (what they'll create)

### Quantity
4-6 exercises spanning 4 hours (include breaks).

### Evaluation Lens
For each exercise, assess:
- Clarity (can facilitator explain in <2 min?)
- Engagement (active participation or passive listening?)
- Output Quality (produces usable artifact?)
- Time Realism (achievable in allocated time?)
```

## QUALITY CHECKLIST

Before deploying a Creative-Context payload:

- [ ] Persona includes creative credentials and domain expertise
- [ ] Inspiration section provides concrete, diverse references
- [ ] Anti-patterns clearly defined (as important as positive examples)
- [ ] Boundaries are hard constraints, freedoms genuinely encouraged
- [ ] Objective is directional but not prescriptive
- [ ] Tone/voice specification is specific, not generic adjectives
- [ ] Output format supports evaluation and iteration
- [ ] Success is not binary (room for gradient of quality)

## ADVANCED TECHNIQUES

### Constraint-Based Creativity

Paradoxically, creativity thrives under constraints. Use:

- **Format constraints:** "Explain in haiku", "Use only monosyllabic words"
- **Perspective constraints:** "From the POV of a skeptical engineer"
- **Resource constraints:** "With $0 budget", "In 280 characters"

### Diverge-Then-Converge Structure

```
1. Divergence Phase (60% of time)
   - Generate quantity without judgment
   - Encourage wild ideas
   - No evaluation

2. Convergence Phase (40% of time)
   - Apply evaluation criteria
   - Cluster and synthesize
   - Prioritize and refine
```

### Provocation Techniques

- **Reversal:** "Design the WORST possible [X]" then invert
- **Random Input:** "Incorporate concepts from [unrelated domain]"
- **Exaggeration:** "What if we had 100x the budget/time/users?"
